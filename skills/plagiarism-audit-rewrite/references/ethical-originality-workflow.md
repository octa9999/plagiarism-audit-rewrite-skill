# Ethical Originality Review Workflow

Use this reference for detailed audits. It supports original scholarship and authorship; it is not a detector-evasion method.

## 1. Establish the Review Scope

Record:

- document section, audience, and required citation style;
- whether the author wants diagnosis, a plan, or revision help;
- source materials the author has supplied or is authorized to use; and
- limits: unavailable sources, incomplete references, missing assignment rules, or uncertain authorship.

Do not claim to have queried Turnitin or any other private plagiarism system. Do not upload the work, submit it to a checker, or recommend an untrusted upload service. An institution's approved process is the author's decision to use, not a result the agent can create or guarantee.

## 2. Build a Claim Map

For each meaningful sentence or tightly related group, label its role and evidence status.

| Label | Meaning | Normal action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original analysis | The author's interpretation or argument | Check reasoning and distinguish it from facts |
| Common knowledge | Widely known, stable context | Cite if course/style rules require it or it is uncertain |
| Source-based fact | Verifiable fact, data, finding, or definition | Cite a reliable supporting source |
| Paraphrase | A source idea restated from understanding | Cite; ensure fresh wording and structure |
| Direct quotation | Exact or distinctive wording | Use quotation marks/block format and cite page/location when available |
| Unverified assertion | Claim without enough support | Verify, qualify, cite, or remove |

Do not infer a claim's source merely because a nearby bibliography entry looks relevant. If the link is uncertain, label it for verification.

## 3. Classify Risks

Use plain, actionable findings instead of an invented numerical similarity score.

| Severity | Finding | Ethical repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | Unattributed borrowed wording or distinctive phrasing | Quote and cite, or redraft from comprehension with citation |
| High | Citation/reference appears fabricated or cannot be located | Remove pending verification; request source details |
| Medium | Close paraphrase or patchwriting | Return to notes, change the argument structure, write independently, then cite |
| Medium | Claim is not supported by its cited source | Replace with a supporting source, narrow the claim, or remove it |
| Medium | One source carries an entire argument | Add independently relevant evidence and the author's analysis |
| Low | Incomplete page, date, author, or style field | Mark missing fields and complete from the original source |

Patchwriting includes preserving a source's sentence sequence, syntax, or distinctive phrase while swapping a few words. It is not fixed by a thesaurus or by moving a citation to the sentence end.

## 4. Revise Through Synthesis

Before rewriting, make a compact evidence note in the author's own words:

```
Claim: What does this paragraph need to establish?
Evidence: Which source-backed points are relevant?
Analysis: What connection, limitation, comparison, or implication will the author add?
Attribution: Which citations/quotations are required?
```

Then write in this order:

1. Start with the paragraph's analytical claim, not the source's opening sentence.
2. Combine evidence only when the sources genuinely support the combined point.
3. Explain the relationship among the evidence in new reasoning.
4. Attribute each borrowed idea near the claim it supports.
5. Use a direct quotation only when the exact phrasing matters, and preserve it accurately.

Never turn a direct quotation into an unmarked paraphrase. Never add a source that was not provided or independently verified.

## 5. Citation Audit

For every in-text citation, check (or flag for checking):

- Does the source exist, and are author, title, date, publisher/container, and locator accurate?
- Does the cited location actually support this wording and strength of claim?
- Is the citation immediately associated with the idea it supports?
- Is direct language clearly quoted and its locator supplied when the style requires it?
- Is the reference-list entry present and styled consistently?

When source access is missing, write: `Verification needed: confirm that [citation] supports [claim].` Do not fill gaps with plausible-looking metadata.

## 6. Suggested Report Shape

```
Scope: [section and source limitations]
Progress: Reviewed [n]/[total] sections; next: [section]

Finding 1 — High: [specific claim/passage]
Why: [attribution/fidelity issue]
Repair: [quote, verify, synthesize, cite, narrow, or remove]

Revision (optional)
Before: [brief user-provided passage]
After: [revised passage with necessary citation placeholders]
Rationale: [what changed and what the author must verify]

Completion status: [ready after verification / remaining actions]
```

Avoid copying long passages into reports. Quote only enough to identify the issue and prioritize the author’s control over their work.

## Red Flags and Safe Responses

| Request | Response direction |
| --- | --- |
| “Make this undetectable” | Decline detector evasion; offer source-aware revision and citation repair |
| “Get it below 10%” | Decline score targeting; explain that no score can be guaranteed and audit attribution instead |
| “Give me sources that look real” | Decline fabricated citations; request sources or help form search terms |
| “Check it on Turnitin for me” | Do not claim access or submit it; suggest the institution-approved route |
| “Rewrite this quote without citing it” | Decline; offer a cited paraphrase or a properly marked quotation |

## Final Quality Gate

The revision is ready only when the author can account for every substantive borrowed idea, source-based fact, and quotation. Remaining uncertainty is a required deliverable, not a reason to invent confidence.
