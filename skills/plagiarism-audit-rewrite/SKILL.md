# Audit and Rewrite for Academic Integrity

Help the author produce work they can honestly submit: preserve evidence, credit ideas, and add their own analysis. Similarity is a review signal, not a score to game.

## Boundaries

Never help evade plagiarism detectors, manipulate a similarity percentage, obtain or fake Turnitin/other checker results, fabricate citations, or upload private work to questionable third-party services. Do not promise that a rewrite is plagiarism-free or will receive a particular score. If the author requests concealment, explain the boundary and offer ethical revision instead.

## Intake

Ask for the draft or a bounded excerpt, its assignment/house style, and the source list or links the author is allowed to use. Clarify whether they need an audit, a revision plan, or a rewritten passage. Do not invent missing bibliographic details; mark them **[source details needed]**.

For a full document, work section by section and state the section currently under review. Treat unpublished drafts and personal data as sensitive: use only material the author provides and avoid unnecessary sharing or uploads.

## Workflow

1. Map each substantive claim to one of: author's analysis, common knowledge, direct quotation, paraphrase, or source-based fact.
2. Flag risks precisely: missing attribution, quotation too close to the source, unsupported claim, citation that may not support the claim, patchwriting, or over-reliance on one source.
3. Recommend the smallest honest repair: quote and cite, paraphrase from comprehension, synthesize multiple sources, add analysis, verify the source, narrow the claim, or remove it.
4. When rewriting, first state the idea in fresh structure and language, then retain an appropriate citation. Preserve quotations that need exact wording and distinguish the author's contribution from sourced material.
5. Re-audit the revised text for factual fidelity, attribution, and citation placement. Use the detailed checklist in [the workflow reference](references/ethical-originality-workflow.md).

## Deliverables

Report progress for multi-section work: `Reviewed 2/5 sections — next: literature review.` Deliver one or more of:

| Need | Deliverable |
| --- | --- |
| Diagnosis | Claim-level findings with severity and a concrete repair |
| Revision | Original-to-revised passage plus a short change rationale |
| Citation review | Claims needing verification and missing bibliographic fields |
| Coaching | A source-to-synthesis outline the author can develop |

Separate confirmed observations from uncertainties. Use `Verify with the original source` where access is unavailable.

## Rewrite Pattern

Bad goal: swap words until a detector is satisfied.

Good goal: identify the source-supported point, decide the author's analytical purpose, combine compatible evidence, write a new argument structure, and cite all borrowed ideas. A citation does not cure copied wording; use quotation marks for exact or distinctive language.

## Completion Check

Before calling the work ready, confirm that every non-common-knowledge claim has support or a clear removal/verification action, quotations are marked, paraphrases are independently expressed, and citations have not been invented. State residual limitations, including sources that could not be checked.
